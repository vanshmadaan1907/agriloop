SECTION 01 • OCTOBER–NOVEMBER HARVEST WINDOW

1. The National Crisis: Paddy Straw & Seasonal Stubble Burning

Every year after the rice harvest (October–November), farmers across Northern India—primarily in Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh—burn tens of millions of tonnes of leftover paddy straw. This occurs because the time window between harvesting paddy and sowing wheat is extremely short (10 to 20 days). Farmers need a rapid, low-cost way to clear fields, and burning remains the fastest method available.

Ramification 01

Severe Seasonal Air Pollution

PM2.5 and PM10 levels surge across the Indo-Gangetic Plain, triggering severe health emergencies, respiratory distress, and annual closures of schools and businesses.

Indo-Gangetic Plain Impact AQI 450+ Severe
Ramification 02

Soil Nutrient Depletion

High burning temperatures sterilize the upper soil layer, destroying essential organic carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, micro-organisms, and beneficial earthworms.

Topsoil Sterilization N • P • C Lost
Ramification 03

Policy Deadlock & Economic Loss

Punitive measures, fines, and machinery subsidies have largely failed because smallholder farmers cannot afford expensive individual machinery, and no organized supply chain exists to buy loose straw.

Supply Chain Gap Zero Buyer Network

SECTION 02 • RESIDUE-COLLECTION-AS-A-SERVICE

2. The Core Solution: Residue-Collection-as-a-Service

AgriLoop re-imagines crop residue not as agricultural waste, but as a valuable industrial commodity. Instead of selling heavy machinery to individual farmers, AgriLoop provides an autonomous 'Residue-Collection-as-a-Service' model.

THE AGRILOOP VALUE PROPOSITION

AgriLoop dispatches fleets of small, autonomous collection robots directly to post-harvest fields. The robots rake, compress, and bale straw in minutes. The farmer pays nothing for the service and receives an immediate cash payout per ton collected, making non-burning significantly more profitable and convenient than burning.

Service Workflow
  • 1. Swarm Dispatched to Field
  • 2. Rake, Compress & Bale in Mins
  • 3. ₹0 Service Fee to Farmer
  • 4. Instant UPI Cash / Ton

SECTION 04 • END-TO-END TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

4. In-Depth System Architecture

AgriLoop combines field swarm hardware, orbital crop-maturity intelligence, dialect-native farmer voice interfaces, and a verified carbon/biomass B2B clearinghouse.

SUBSYSTEM A

A. Residue Robots ('Loopbots')

Loopbots are compact electric tracked robots built with lightweight chassis to prevent deep soil sinking. Each unit features active rake and baling mechanisms, LiDAR and stereo cameras for obstacle detection, and GPS-RTK for precise centimeter-level field traversal.

Swarm Supervision Architecture

Teams of 5–10 Loopbots work together under a centralized Supervisor Vehicle that carries replacement battery banks and hauls baled residue.

NAVIGATION GPS-RTK + LiDAR
SWARM UNIT SIZE 5–10 Loopbots / Truck
SUBSYSTEM B + FIGURE 3 ISRO / Sentinel Optical & Radar Pipeline

B. AI Harvest-Prediction Engine

Using satellite optical and radar imagery combined with combine-harvester movement logs, the AI model calculates crop drying curves and maturity dates across entire agricultural belts. It dynamically assigns robot swarms to fields with strict priority given to imminent sowing deadlines.

SECTION 05 • ENGINEERING RESILIENCE

5. Technical Risk Matrix & Mitigations

Every real-world rural and agronomic constraint is paired with a specific hardware, edge-AI, or financial engineering mitigation.

CHALLENGE 01 • FIELD TERRAIN

Muddy & soft paddy field conditions

AgriLoop Engineering Mitigation

Lightweight tracked chassis (<3 psi ground pressure); AI pathfinding avoids waterlogged field zones.

CHALLENGE 02 • POWER & UPTIME

Limited battery life during peak harvest

AgriLoop Engineering Mitigation

Swappable battery modularity; solar canopy charging station integrated into supervisor truck.

CHALLENGE 03 • LAND GEOMETRY

Small & irregular land holdings

AgriLoop Engineering Mitigation

Swarm architecture allows compact, agile robots to navigate tight field boundaries easily.

CHALLENGE 04 • RURAL TRUST

Low initial technology adoption/trust

AgriLoop Engineering Mitigation

Zero upfront farmer cost, fast UPI payouts, and localized voice-based onboarding.

CHALLENGE 05 • FLEET FINANCING

High capital expenditure of fleet

AgriLoop Engineering Mitigation

Fleet leasing model financed via B2B biomass sales, carbon offset credits, and clean-tech grants.

CHALLENGE 06 • RURAL TELECOM

Weak rural cellular network coverage

AgriLoop Engineering Mitigation

Onboard edge AI computing for navigation; batch syncing when within supervisor truck coverage.
